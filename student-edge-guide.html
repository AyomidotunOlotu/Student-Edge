02 — Quick Wins

Things you can start this week

Design
🎨

Canva is not a cheat code — it's a starting gun

Most students learn Canva then do nothing with it. Charge ₦2,000–₦5,000 per flyer for student fellowships, departmental associations, or small businesses near campus. One client a week = ₦8,000–₦20,000/month from your phone.

Reselling
📱

Gadget reselling is the most underrated campus business

Earphones, chargers, cables, phone cases. Buy in bulk from Okafor Market or Jiji, sell on WhatsApp status. Students break these things weekly. ₦500 margin × 20 items = ₦10,000 moved from your hostel room with zero shop rent.

Content
📹

Faceless YouTube is real — but slow. Here's what to expect

Your first 3 videos will get 0–50 views. That's normal. The students making ₦50k/month from it started 9–18 months ago. Pick a niche (Nigerian student tips, study hacks, budget cooking), post consistently, and monetize through AdSense + sponsorships once you hit 1,000 subs.

Freelancing
✍️

Copywriting: the skill with the fastest return on campus

Every business needs words — product descriptions, captions, WhatsApp broadcasts. You don't need a degree. Study 3 books (Ogilvy on Advertising, Ca$hvertising, This is Marketing), practice on real Nigerian brands, and charge ₦5,000–₦15,000 per project within 60 days.

Academic
📚

The 3-day rule before every exam

Don't start studying the night before. Your brain needs at least 3 short sessions to consolidate a topic into long-term memory. The students who pass big aren't smarter — they started earlier and used past questions as their primary revision tool, not textbooks.

Networking
🤝

Your 100-person network is more valuable than 10,000 followers

Focus on building 100 deep, genuine relationships before graduation — classmates, lecturers, seniors, business owners near campus. These are the people who will refer you for jobs, give you contracts, and vouch for your character. Social media numbers mean nothing without offline trust.

Campus Hustle

The real campus hustle map: what earns, what doesn't

Not all campus businesses are equal. Here's an honest breakdown of what actually makes consistent money on a Nigerian university campus — tested, not theoretical.

  • Print/photocopy runs: Low margin but consistent. Partner with a business centre, collect WhatsApp orders, mark up 30–50%. Scales with volume, not effort.
  • Course material packs: The winner. Compile past questions + summarised notes for your department. Charge ₦500–₦2,000 per PDF. One file, infinite resales. Students ask every semester.
  • Gadget accessories: Fast turnover, low capital. Earphones from ₦800 → sell for ₦1,500. Focus on items with a clear reorder pattern — students will always buy chargers and cables.
  • Tutoring: High value, referral-driven. Start with your strongest subject. One student tells two others. Price at ₦2,000–₦5,000/session. GST courses, calculus, and anatomy are hot year-round.
  • Event photography: ₦5,000–₦25,000 per event. Departmental weeks, birthdays, convos. You need: a decent phone, SnapSeed, and the confidence to say yes first, then improve.
Academics

How to pass well without reading everything

The brutal truth: most lecturers test 20% of the course content 80% of the time. Find the 20% and you're already winning. Here's the exact system:

  • Get 5 years of past questions first. Before touching a textbook, scan what has been repeated. Those are your core topics. Build everything else around them.
  • Read in active recall mode. Don't highlight. Cover what you read and try to reproduce it. If you can't, re-read. If you can, move. This doubles retention compared to passive reading.
  • Find your peak hours and protect them. Most people do their best thinking between 6–10am. That's 4 hours before the campus chaos starts. Use it. One focused hour beats three distracted ones.
  • Form a 3-person study group — not 10. Large groups become social events. Three people: you review, they question, you defend. That's where real learning happens.
  • Don't neglect CA scores. In most Nigerian universities, continuous assessment is 30–40% of your final grade. Quiet points. Students who miss CA work twice as hard in exams for the same result.
Digital Income

Earning in dollars from a campus hostel — the real path

It's possible. It's not easy. And it doesn't happen in 2 weeks. Here's what actually works for Nigerian students with limited equipment and intermittent internet:

  • Start with a skill that has low tech requirements. Copywriting, social media management, and basic graphic design can be done on a mid-range Android phone with mobile data. Don't wait for a laptop.
  • Fiverr is slow, direct outreach is faster. Build a simple portfolio, DM small foreign businesses on Instagram or LinkedIn, explain what you can do, and offer a free sample. One client who pays $50 is worth 50 ₦1,000 gigs locally.
  • Sort your payment method before you need it. Grey, Chipper Cash, or a verified Payoneer account. Clients won't wait while you figure out how to receive money. This is the part most beginners skip and regret.
  • Pick a niche within your skill. Not "graphic designer" — "social media graphics for fitness coaches." Specificity makes you memorable and easier to refer. The riches are genuinely in the niches here.
